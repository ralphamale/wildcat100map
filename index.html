Runner tips

Practical preparation and race-day guidance distilled from Wildcat runner experiences.

Think one lap at a time

Break the race into 2.5-mile decisions. Expect camp to become a recurring temptation to stop.

Use the loop—don’t linger

Exploit frequent access to aid and gear, but keep each staging-area visit deliberate and short.

Prepare for serious heat

Heat-acclimate, start conservatively, and use ice or another active-cooling routine.

Hydrate on a schedule

Drink every lap instead of waiting for thirst. Use only electrolytes already tested in training.

Build a personal pit

Stage a cooler, ice, familiar fuel, dry gear, and lap-sized supply kits beside the loop.

Use crew or a pacer

Practical help and encouragement matter. If running solo, schedule outside check-ins.

Problem-solve early

Stay positive and address heat, stomach, foot, or pacing problems before they compound.

Protect your feet

Expect wet grass or rain. Bring many socks, towels, blister supplies, and a second footwear option.

Train the actual surface

Prepare for uneven, cambered grass. Walk the sloped section early if it irritates a knee or hip.

Fuel consistently

Use a repeatable lap-based plan and keep indispensable, familiar foods in your own supplies.

Prepare for the night

Carry a strong headlamp, a backup light, spare batteries, and bug spray.
